# Marvin Studio — 3D edition

Three.js geometry replaces the previous MP4 entrance. The model uses the original booth composition plus the supplied black transparent Marvin wordmark, olive velvet fabric and six photos extracted directly from `ape.pdf`. It faces directly forward, with a warm yellow backlit top sign, reflective champagne metal trim, a compact recessed stainless printer tray and an empty interior without a chair. The curtain has irregular soft folds and a gentle continuous breeze. Depth is inferred from the references.

Use the Node 24 release pinned in `../.nvmrc` (validated locally with 24.19.0).

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

The site opens straight on the photobooth. In front of the booth a big "Scroll" button (an ink pill with a down arrow in an ivory key, made like EXIT and "Book an event") shows until you start walking in; tapping it walks in, the same as scrolling.

Vite serves the website on port 5173. A single downward wheel gesture, upward touch swipe or PageDown starts a 3.6-second entrance: one continuous zoom while the curtain opens, with the portfolio page sitting behind the doorway and growing with the camera, so there is no white flash or fade; the booth's back wall is a depth-only “portal” through which the HTML page shows. An upward wheel gesture at the portfolio's top reverses home at the same slower pace. Scrolling within portfolio content remains native; “Our work” is an immediate shortcut. The entrance occupies one viewport of document scroll and can also be scrubbed with the scrollbar. A continuous forward/lateral path and logarithmic distance interpolation keep the approach from rushing near the curtain. Gentle wind continues at rest with the top rail pinned, capped at 30 fps and paused when hidden or beyond the entrance. The entrance ignores the OS reduced-motion setting. No video is requested. The renderer is dynamically imported only when needed, with a capped pixel ratio and resource cleanup. Missing WebGL and context loss retain a matching poster fallback.

Before entering, the booth is seen in a three-quarter view on a lit studio floor with a soft backdrop, a glowing sign and warm light spilling from inside (`src/scene/createBoothScene.ts`, `studioRig.ts`); the view drifts slightly with the pointer. Edit geometry and supplied texture crops in `src/scene/buildBooth.ts` (including the photo-strip display beside the curtain; the curtain length is `curtainDrop` in `src/scene/curtainMotion.ts`), cloth response and the hover “hand” in `src/scene/curtainMotion.ts` / `src/scene/createBoothScene.ts`, lighting/framing in `src/scene/createBoothScene.ts`, and choreography in `src/lib/timeline.ts`.

Inside the booth you first face the back wall, then the view turns left (a CSS 3D room, `.room` in `src/styles/site.css`) to the wall where the monitor hangs, with the console (selfie button and print slot) below it (`src/components/PortfolioMonitor.tsx`, `SelfiePrinter.tsx`); everything there is sized from one unit (`--u` in `src/styles/site.css`), so it looks the same on every device and only scales. The monitor shows one event at a time as Photos, a playable 3D model or a Video. The photos ride round a ring you can drag or spin with the arrows; the small arrows and dots by the corner change event. One wheel notch or a short swipe walks you in. Once inside, the page is steady: the window is locked and the booth wall scrolls on its own frame, from the monitor down to the booking section only; the EXIT button or the logo walks you back out. Events (Streetscape 2026 at IKEA, B&K's Wedding, Noera's 8th Anniversary), their photos, videos and 3D models live in `src/data/portfolio.ts`: the 3D model is the IKEA event's own printed booth (`public/portfolio/booths/`), or for the wedding and Noera their backdrop on its own (`public/portfolio/backdrops/`; the wedding's a cut-out cloth, Noera's butterfly wall a rigid board). Each event's photos are `public/portfolio/events/<event>-01.webp`… (cropped 5:6, 600 × 720); they start loading a moment after the opening screen has, unless the page opened inside the booth, and a photo that fails is asked for again before it says "Photo unavailable". Its clip and poster are in `public/portfolio/videos/`. The selfie button flashes (with a real DSLR shutter recording, `public/sounds/shutter.mp3`: "Camera Shutter" by caseymoura on freesound.org under CC BY 3.0, see `public/sounds/NOTICE.txt`; the credit is kept in that notice (served with the site at `sounds/NOTICE.txt`); played in `src/lib/sounds.ts` as `<audio>` so phones in silent mode still hear it) and pushes a polaroid out of the black slot on the console, printed with the next of the real booth shots in `public/portfolio/selfies/` (`selfies` in `src/data/portfolio.ts`); it develops from dark to full colour and, three seconds after it is out, flips and spins away by itself. These selfie effects and the entrance play regardless of the OS reduced-motion setting. Scrolling down leaves the booth for the booking section: "Book for an event" opens WhatsApp, with Instagram and TikTok links. "Book a session" (in the header and in the booking section, or straight away from an address ending in `?book`) opens a full-screen form for a session at the walk-in booth (`src/components/BookSession.tsx`): strips, the "All your photos" add-on, the guest's name and whether they are at the booth now. The site has no payment server, so "Book and pay" opens WhatsApp with the order written out; the staff reply with the QRIS and the code. Prices and the address are in `booking` in `src/data/portfolio.ts` (set `extraStrip` once the price of extra strips is known). Fill in the WhatsApp number and the two profile links in `social` in `src/data/portfolio.ts`. Logos are `public/textures/marvin-wordmark.png` (monitor) and `public/textures/marvin-logo-studio.png` (header). Asset provenance and the Codex skill adaptation are recorded in `../docs/models/assets.md`.

Under the monitor, before "Book for an event", Marvin's prints stand on slowly turning white displays (`src/components/PrintsShowcase.tsx` and `src/components/ProductTurntable.tsx`, `.prints` in `src/styles/site.css`): the 4R print on a clear acrylic easel, the XL strip (75 × 25 cm) in its clear sleeve hanging from a chrome stand and the mini tote (25 × 20 cm) with its printed card inside, as 3D models (`src/scene/createProductScene.ts`, three.js). The stand turns slowly all the time; dragging sideways turns it by hand and the arrow keys bring a print round to the front, whose name is above. What is printed on each is `src` of `products` in `src/data/portfolio.ts` (`public/portfolio/products/`: real prints from Marvin's booths, the 4R from Streetscape 2026 at IKEA, the XL strip from Jetour and the tote card from Noera's Eightfinity); the artwork is drawn in its own colours, unlit and not tone mapped, so the photos stay sharp and true. The booth's cream wall fades into a warm white, the prints rise in with the scroll, and the booking section slides over the end of it as a white sheet with rounded top corners. Its models load once it is near and are drawn only while it is on screen.

"Book an event" in the header (`src/components/SiteHeader.tsx`, `.book-event`, made like the EXIT button: an ink pill with Marvin's star in an ivory key) never skips the booth: from in front of the booth it walks in, turns to the monitor and then glides down to "Book for an event"; inside the booth it simply glides down (`bookFlowEvent` in `src/lib/sections.ts`, handled in `src/hooks/useJourneyProgress.ts`). On a phone, or a window too narrow for both, it takes the tagline's place.

On a phone the photos on the monitor are about half the screen wide (`--phone-card`), and the monitor uses more of the screen's height.
